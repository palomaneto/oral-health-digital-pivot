# Case Study: Identifying At-Risk Dental Patients Using Medical History Data

## Background
Preventive oral healthcare is closely linked to a patient’s wider medical history. 
Individuals with chronic conditions such as diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or limited access to routine care are at increased risk of poor oral health outcomes.

This project simulates how structured patient data can be used to identify at-risk individuals and support earlier intervention.

## Problem
Dental services often collect patient data, but it is not always analysed in a way that highlights oral health risk early enough.
This can lead to:
- Missed opportunities for prevention
- Increased emergency appointments
- Poor long-term outcomes for vulnerable patients

## Approach
A synthetic dataset was created to represent dental patients with varying:
- Age
- Gender
- Medical history
- Oral health risk level
- Last dental visit date

The dataset was analysed using SQL to:
- Identify patients with high oral health risk
- Highlight vulnerable groups based on medical history
- Support data-driven decision-making in dental services

## Data Used
The dataset contains:
- 200 synthetic patient records
- No real patient data
- Structured fields suitable for SQL analysis

Key fields include:
- patient_id
- age
- gender
- medical_history
- oral_health_risk
- last_visit_date

## Outcome
The project demonstrates how:
- clinical knowledge from dental nursing
- health informatics principles
- basic data analysis skills
can be combined to support preventive oral healthcare and population health insights.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Health informatics thinking
- Data structuring and documentation
- SQL-based data analysis
- Understanding of oral health risk factors
- Clear communication of findings
